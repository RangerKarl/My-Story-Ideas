The galley lights glowed steady as the ship rumbled on its chartered flight pan. The mercenary lance known as The Briarpatch Brigade was seated at a little foldout table.

"So, *boss*, " said Sergeant Lillia Santiago, "what's the plan now?". Her face, an exemplar of every no-nonsense Taurian belle, was scrunched up in parts mockery and frustration.

The two other Lancemates, Corporals Andy White and Margrette Reed, kept looking between their senior second-in-command, and their Lance Commander. 1st Lieutenant Mitchell Devereaux was nursing a cup of black coffee, with a noteputer in one hand. He was making a valiant effort to concentrate on the little device.

"Because," continued Lillia, "that last run was a real goat-rope, Mitch! We nearly got our rides shot out from under us, and I bet when we get back to Herotitus the repair bill is going to blow out our budget! I should know, Mitch, seeing as I'm the one that does the book keeping!" Her voice was, while not up there with the best drill instructors of the Taurian Confederacy Armed Forces, starting to peak in the same way a kettle warns you it's boiling.

"Lillia, I don't see the issue. We've gotten the industrial recon data, as requested by our client. That's a Mission Success in my books, so once we get back, hit up the HPG and phone home we should be seeing money in the bank."

"A two-bit scanjob in the ass-end of the Inner Sphere is not going to pay for a new PPC, Mitch. Andy's down his main weapon on his Panther, which makes him a little too much like a paperweight for me. And my own Jenner pretty much got its leg shot out from underneath it." Lillia exclaimed. "Every last contract since we all mustered out of the Kell Hounds has been skin of our teeth. That training detail near \<INSERT PLANET HERE> was a great breather, but then you had to cut that contract short too."

"As I recall, Lillia, you were on board when we decided that."

"E-even so, that was hoping we'd hit it big! Right now we've got naught to show for 'cept a bank statement that hovers near the redline as much as Margrette's Commando when she fires off her lasers, and a whole boatload of repair bills! I'm serious Mitch, if we don't set up a real big score... I'm gonna have to break off."

Mitchell laughed, a coughing bark as the coffee he was sipping took a detour down the wrong end.

"Lillia, the only reason we're bunched up together is that there isn't anyone hiring now. There hasn't been a big one in the region, not since the Mariks started going up against each other. And the Taurians don't look like they're gonna be busting anyone's balls here. This is the Aurigan Reach, Lillia. Nothing happens here worth a damn." 

As Lillia was gearing up for her rebuttal, Andy decided to speak up.

"SGT Lillia's right. We signed on for good consistent work, but lately it feels like we're getting screwed on the upkeep. We need to find a better payday."

Margrette nodded, her ginger pigtail flapping in the stacatto breeze of the galley's air exchangers.

Mitchell sighed. "Okay, if it's going to be focused fire, let me look through my 'dex and-"

The ship shuddered in a slightly different way. The main master channel clacked on, and the ship's XO spoke up.

"This is the XO. We have detected unusual debris ahead in our flight path. All hands prepare for zero-g and rapid burn operations. All non-flight personnel, secure your stations. I don't want any flying 'Mech actuators."

The tannoy clicked off. 

Mitchell muttered. "What the hell is going on now?"

When I woke up from a dreamless slumber, I was expecting my phone's alarm clock to be the one doing so. This weird, droning monotone beep was not it.

When I opened my eyes, I was expecting my bedroom, with the dim lights on my extension cords and phones providing some lighting. I did not expect the lights to be coming from a single glass pane, not 5 inches away from my face.

When I made to move my arms, I expected to be able to do so. Instead, my arms seemed to bump into the walls of what was rapidly resembling a coffin of some sort. 

I wriggled a bit. 

*The fuck is this? Am I dreaming?*

Rotating my head to the right, I could see a bank of lights with labels on them. The one saying EXTERNAL ATMO seemed to be bright red, as well as the one saying SUIT INTF OK. I couldn't really feel much else. Whatever material I had against my back felt like any other automotive padding I've ever sat in or on. 

Peering out of the tiny window I could almost make out that the stars were slowly moving in one direction, or rather one plane of movement. 

*Am I spinning? Why am I spinning?*

I ran my hands around the walls closest to them. I mostly only felt smooth metal or composite stuff, but then my left hand fumbled on some sort of switch.

As I clicked it, another light came on. The blinking diode next to the label saying "BEACON" ran green. 

I had no idea where I was. There was no way this was home, 2026 Earth had basically no space presence, and besides, who the heck would kidnap a 40 year old IT guy and stuff him in a space pod? 

The thought of me being involved in one of those silly yarns I always kept reading on company time came to mind. Portal fantasies were cool and all, but the thing about that is that they were fantasies first! 

The notion of being kidnapped by some extraversal joker and thrown into some strange world was outlandish as hell, and I had no time for that.

I hoped to any power above that there was a cheat involved. To my eternal joy and anguish, there was.
