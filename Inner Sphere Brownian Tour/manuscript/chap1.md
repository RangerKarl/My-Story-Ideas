## Morning Mirage

### **Kuala Lumpur, Present Day **

A dark bedroom, with a game/workstation in one corner, a mess of random cables strewn around.

*click-creak*

The door opens, and a man stepped inside. Without even turning on the lights, he drops his backpack at the foot of the bed

With a deep sigh, he falls on the mattress, disturbing the unmade blanket also by the foot of the bed. He lies, facing the ceiling. Another tedious day of IT requests at work.

He digs out his phone. Fiddles around. More work emails and messages filtering in. Fuck it. He took off early today because the knot in his head, that he'd been dodging for 10 years now, has finally come back. Should have put in that request for leave.

He fishes out his phone from his pocket, a small crater in the reinforced touchscreen the biggest mark on the beaten up device. Unlocking it, he starts scrolling on the typical social media sites, before sighing and random browsing mecha fan forums and sites.

A turn of the head to look at his newest (and only) toy. A VF-31A Kairos, sitting on top of his PC, "tom yam" gamer lights strobing. He hasn't fixed the lighting yet. Probably won't for a while. Throwing on a *kain pelikat* over the top of the case was good enough. 

It was a really cool toy, if only it kept its pose a little better, had some more doodads. For the price, he couldn't complain.

He wishes he was at the controls. Flight sims were great, but nobody really captured the fantasy of being in the cockpit of a beauty like that, as depicted in anime anyway. In real life such things were impossible.

He lives in a tedious world of gray.

*The haze is really getting to me, huh...*

A Discord message from someone pops up. Notifications were turned off, that shouldn't have been possible. If he was thinking more clearly, if the air was cleaner, he might have just swatted off the toast note.

Instead he taps on the damn thing.

A user with a random string of characters. Obvious bot. But instead of the typical spam message about interesting avatars or art commissions, it was a message.

```
You seem like you could use a little more than a THRILL in your life! click here <<omnigacha.com>>
```

Again, if he had a clearer head or cleaner air, maybe he wouldn't have clicked that.

But in the state of bottomed out ennui, his finger almost automatically floated over the hyperlink, and clicked it.

The phone suddenly glowed with like, a billion Lumens, but the man wasn't disturbed. 

Because the man wasn't there in his bed any more.

The phone plopped onto the mattress.

Shaking off the veritable storm of notifactions, I took stock of my current position. Instead of my nice comfortable bed, wearing my work shirt and slacks, I was at the seat of a fighter plane, or I supposed, an actual VF-11B Variable Fighter. 

My heart was torn between absolutely *geeking out* and pitching the right hand stick down to pivot into a mountain in panic. Wherever I was, I was pretty high up, but not so high I couldn't see the ground.

I gave the fusion-powered rocket fighter robot plane thing a command to roll gently, letting me see over the lip of the OTM material cockpit bucket. 

At that point I noticed the rough material of the seat against my thighs. Looking down I realized...

*Why am I in a singlet and boxers? I don't even own any boxer shorts!*

At least I was also belted down, some rather thin straps forming a 5-point harness over my torso and waist. Without an actual flight suit this would not be a fun time to bring into a fight, but I could fly, and I could operate the controls.

She flew like a dream, the Heads Up Display holo projected onto the front section of the transparent canopy telling me I was flying at 1100 KPH, at an altitude above ground level of five thousand meters. It had seemingly stuck to altitude...ah. I noticed a couple switches to the left of the massive multifunctional display that were related to altitude and attitude hold.

*Well, if it's anything like the DCS Hornet...*

I just gave the stick a good yank back and to the right. That seemed to trip the on-board AI to release the attitude hold, letting the plane's vector free up. I was now fully controlling the bird.

Understanding the relationship between stick and onboard flight control, I put it in a rough bank, and set attitude/altitude controls to a nice lazy pylon turn. I didn't see anything complaining about fuel, so I guess the thing about overtechnology thermonuclear turbines heating air for almost free was still true.

*Now, let's see about landing this somewhere in the mean time.*

The little village of Little Furrow, up by the Scarhill Forest range, was in trouble. Out of nowhere, a band of miscreants with mismatched rags, and far too many small arms had surrounded them. The few armed villagers, AgroTech retirees with M&G Hunting Rifles and haybale forks were outmatched by men with automatic weapons and hungry looks in their eyes.

They had come just as the village was winding down for the evening. One of the shophouses was burning, a victim of a grenade launcher loaded with incendiary ammo. Only its distance away from the other buildings saved the rest of the town from burning up, but the impromptu pyre put out a massive column of smoke in the air.

All the villagers were rounded up and hauled to the middle of the viillage square. Surrounding them, dirty bandits, men and women alike looked at them like wolves would look at sheep. One of them stepped up, proclaiming, "I can't wait! I'm gonna have me a bit of that one!", gesturing lewdly at a plump middle aged lady.

The lady fainted at the blatant threat to her body. Behind her, a young man, on the cusp of 16, managed to holder her up with his lanky body. He glowered at the belligerent man, angered that he wasn't able to do anything except wait out the inevitable.

A shot rang out with a thunder and crack! The man grew a pinpoint hole in his chest, that suddenly spurted red before his body dropped to the dirt. The sun shone down on his corpse in that light of the Golden Hour just before dusk. The villagers then heard as one, a terrible stomping sound.

Old Man Wilkins, one of the retiree Agtechs recognized it. He had driven old HarvesterMechs before, and he knew the particular sound of large mechanical feet. He turned, and as everyone else slowly followed his head, they saw IT.

IT was a 'Mech, but festooned with heavy machine gun, and a fighting laser under it's 'chin'. Its legs were reverse-jointed and thin. The tiny cockpit, Armorglass paneling set dead center in the tapered armored torso.

Anyone who had read a Jane's Fighting Machines knew what it was. A *Locust*, the Inner Sphere's most common BattleMech. They did *not* know who the person standing on top of it was, wearing a bungee harness and cradling a rifle.

The person yelled out, a voice too shrill to be anything but female. "MY, FELLOW, BANDITOS! The one I punished here, what was his crime?!"

The bandit gang seemed to also shie away. They too were intimidated by the power under the feet of this person.

As the Mech trodded closer, the villagers could see IT come closer and closer, as it slowed further and further. Soon, its feet were mere handspans away from the huddling group of civilians. IT pitched its torso down, "arm" machine gun mounts lazily drawing circles. Circles to control them.

The person, or rather woman, on top of the Locust spoke again. 

"HIS CRIME, WAS TRYING TO CUT AHEAD WITHOUT THINKING! We can't tolerate *idiots* who just up and lose discipline, in front of their peers. In front of their prey!"

She paced on top of the machine of death. Wearing a MechWarrior's shorts, a string-thin bikini and a loose bandolier of bullets, she almost looked like a model, were it not for the scar that ran down her face and the manic grin she wore over everything else.

She gestured magnanimously. 

"The Dread Pack does not cut ahead of its own! We catch, we kill, we eat as one! Bounty for one is nothing, Bounty for All is everything! You there!-" she pointed at a random bandit -", clean up this mess! We must be presentable for the Honored One...as must our guests."

Suddenly she rappelled off of the locust, a good 8 meters high. With a skillful flip and flick of the wrist she arrested her velocity just enough to land on her feet with elan. 

"Who the hell are you people!" yelled the teen boy, the fire of youthful anger still present.

"Well, well, a fighter yet. I thought this dump was just for old people and losers. Let me turn that around on you kid. Who are you?" said the evident boss lady. "Wait, don't answer that. I don't care. What I am to you people, is that I am your only lifeline out of here, back into the world of the living. Do as I say, you'll live for the Honored One. Do anything else, well...see our friend."

She gestured to the Locust, who seemed to take it as an order to move out of the town. As it moved, she grinned again. "Why don't we have a little fun while we wait for my Master?"

I had managed to settle into the controls well enough that when I had arrived at the point of interest, I was no longer threatening to add another notch to the ground's kill score. In GERWALK mode, I had a pretty low height compared to Battroid mode, so I managed to keep the bulk of my craft below the treeline. 

I managed to toggle my Thud's head turret into peeking up and into what appeared to be a village. On passive multispectral the picture seemed clear as mud. A group of civilian folks, maybe 30-50 of them, were massed together in one blob. Herded in, even. They were surrounded by armed people, and what looked like 1 Battle Pod of some description. The multispectral view didn't have the best picture resolution, and at any rate the sun was in that twilight mode that always disrupted night vision. 

This did not look like some police operation. The belligerents seemed way too undisciplined, breaking rank just to partake of the local bounty. 

*Damn these beasts.*

I had to think things through. Combat as a solo operator was risky at the best of times but I couldn't live with myself if I just let this go. I was given a gift, and damn if I wasn't going to use it!

While I recriminated, the person who had ridden the top of the Pod rappeled down and faced the civs. Her rifle in hand was still warm, after taking that pot shot at one of her own. I didn't have much anthropology, but every loose memory I had said that she was meting out draconian punishment for a military outfit. 

Which made me think this wasn't military, or if it was, it sure seemed to be medieval about things. Space pirates and the like did exist even in Macross 7's time, although the corridor they used didn't have to worry about it. It was increasingly clear I was looking at an example of the local hostis humanis generis.

Killing pirates was probably not a universal good, but whoever it was here was likely not up to any good. I judged my options for a bit. 1 35mm high velocity gunpod with 350 rounds of ammo per magazine, for 3 mags (2 spare in my ECA left shield.). 1 armor-piercing bayonette for the gunpod, in a flip out configuration. Nothing else. I wasn't flying one of the nicer upgrades to the Thud in the late 2050s, a quick scan of the diagnostic readout told me that. No Pinpoint barrier meant I would need to be careful not to incur too much damage. 

The Pod suddenly made to move off.
